Legal Basis of PNPKI

The legal basis ensures that the Philippine National Public Key Infrastructure (PNPKI) operates under established laws and regulations, providing a secure and trustworthy framework for digital transactions. It defines the rights, responsibilities, and obligations of PNPKI users, ensuring compliance with data privacy, cybersecurity, and electronic commerce laws. This legal foundation strengthens the validity and acceptance of digital certificates in government and private sector transactions.

RA 8792​

Electronic Commerce Act

Republic Act No. 8792, or the Electronic Commerce Act of 2000, provides the legal foundation for electronic transactions in the Philippines, including the use of digital certificates issued by PNPKI. It recognizes electronic documents and digital signatures as legally valid, giving them the same weight as handwritten signatures in government and private transactions. This law ensures that PNPKI users can securely sign and authenticate documents online while protecting against cyber threats like hacking and data tampering.

EO No. 810, s. 2009​

Application of Digital Signatures in e-Government Services

Executive Order No. 810, s. 2009 establishes the Philippine National Public Key Infrastructure (PNPKI) to enable secure and trusted online transactions in government and the private sector. It mandates the use of digital certificates to authenticate identities, ensure data integrity, and support e-Government initiatives. This EO strengthens the legal framework for electronic transactions by promoting the adoption of digital signatures, enhancing cybersecurity, and improving efficiency in government services.

A.M. No. 01-7-01-SC​

Rules on Electronic Evidence

Administrative Matter No. 01-7-01-SC, or the Rules on Electronic Evidence, sets the guidelines for the admissibility of electronic documents and digital signatures in Philippine courts. It recognizes that electronic data, including those signed using PNPKI digital certificates, can serve as legal evidence, just like paper-based documents. This ensures that PNPKI users can securely authenticate and submit electronic records in legal proceedings, reinforcing the reliability of digital transactions.

GPPB Resolution 16-2019​

Allow the Use of Digital Signatures in All GPPB Issuances

GPPB Resolution No. 16-2019 allows the use of digital signatures in government procurement processes to enhance security, efficiency, and transparency. It recognizes PNPKI digital certificates as valid for signing procurement documents, ensuring authenticity and integrity in electronic transactions. This supports the shift to e-Government and paperless transactions, reducing risks like forgery and unauthorized alterations.

COA Circular 2021-006​

Principles and Guidelines on the use of digital signatures

COA Circular 2021-006 recognizes the use of digital signatures, including those issued by PNPKI, in government financial transactions and audits. It allows electronic documents signed with PNPKI certificates to be considered valid and legally binding, ensuring authenticity, integrity, and accountability in government records. This circular supports the transition to paperless transactions, improving efficiency while maintaining compliance with auditing and security standards.

ARTA M.C. 2020-06​

Guidelines on the Use of Digital Signatures to Streamline Government Transactions

ARTA Memorandum Circular No. 2020-06 promotes the use of digital signatures, including PNPKI digital certificates, to streamline government transactions and reduce processing time. It mandates agencies to adopt electronic authentication to improve efficiency, security, and transparency in public services. By enabling paperless and contactless transactions, this circular supports faster service delivery while ensuring the integrity and legality of electronic documents.
